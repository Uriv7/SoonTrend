What Is Quarantine? (Definition and Overview)

Quarantine is a public health measure aimed at preventing the spread of infectious diseases. It involves separating individuals who have been exposed to a contagious agent from the general population. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), quarantine can be implemented in various settings, including homes, hospitals, and other isolated areas. In 2020, the WHO reported that over 50,000 people were quarantined globally due to COVID-19. How Does Quarantine Work?

Quarantine involves a series of steps, starting from the moment an individual is suspected of having come into contact with a contagious agent. The first step is to isolate the individual from the general population, either by moving them to a separate area or by restricting their movements. The next step is to monitor the individual for symptoms, which can include fever, cough, and shortness of breath. If symptoms appear, the individual may be transferred to a hospital or a designated quarantine facility. In some cases, quarantine may be accompanied by medical treatment, such as antibiotics or antiviral medications. The goal of quarantine is to prevent the spread of disease, but it's not without risks. Quarantine can lead to emotional distress, social isolation, and even physical harm.
