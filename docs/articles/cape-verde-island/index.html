What Is Cape Verde?

Cape Verde is an archipelago of ten volcanic islands, located off the coast of the Atlantic Ocean. The islands are divided into two groups: the Barlavento islands to the north and the Sotavento islands to the south. With a total area of approximately 4,000 square kilometers, Cape Verde is a relatively small country, but its unique cultural heritage and breathtaking landscapes make it a fascinating destination. According to a study by the World Tourism Organization, Cape Verde has seen a significant increase in tourism over the past decade, with over 700,000 visitors in 2020 alone. The capital city, Praia, is a bustling metropolis with a rich history, vibrant markets, and a stunning waterfront. Cape Verde is a former Portuguese colony, and as such, the official language is Portuguese, but many residents also speak Creole and English. The country has a diverse economy, with a strong focus on tourism, fishing, and agriculture. In recent years, Cape Verde has become a popular destination for ecotourism, with many visitors drawn to its stunning natural beauty and unique wildlife. How Does Cape Verde Work?

Cape Verde is a semi-presidential representative democratic republic, with a president serving as head of state and a prime minister as head of government. The country has a unicameral legislature, with 72 members elected by proportional representation. The government is responsible for providing public services, including education, healthcare, and infrastructure. The economy is driven by a mix of public and private sectors, with a strong focus on tourism, fishing, and agriculture. According to a report by the International Monetary Fund, Cape Verde has made significant progress in recent years in terms of economic development, with a growth rate of over 4% in 2020. The country is also a member of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) and the African Union, which provides access to regional markets and economic cooperation. In terms of infrastructure, Cape Verde has a well-developed transportation network, including several international airports and a number of seaports. The country is also investing heavily in renewable energy, with a goal of generating 50% of its electricity from renewable sources by 2025.
