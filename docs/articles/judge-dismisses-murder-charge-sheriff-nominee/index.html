How Does Self-Defense Work?

In determining whether the use of force is justified, courts consider a range of factors, including the severity of the threat, the availability of alternative means of escape or de-escalation, and the level of force used by the defendant. Common Misconceptions About Self-Defense

There are several common misconceptions about self-defense that can lead to confusion and misinformation. One of the most significant misconceptions is that self-defense requires a high level of physical skill or training. While physical skill can be beneficial, it is not a necessary prerequisite for self-defense. Self-defense can be achieved through a range of means, including verbal de-escalation, physical restraint, or the use of force. Another misconception is that self-defense is only available to individuals who are facing a direct threat. In fact, self-defense can be justified in a range of circumstances, including when an individual is facing a threat from a third party or when they are protecting someone else from harm.
