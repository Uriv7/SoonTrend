What Is Juneteenth: A Definition and Overview

Juneteenth, also known as Freedom Day or Jubilee Day, is a holiday that commemorates the emancipation of enslaved African Americans in the United States. It is observed on June 19th every year and is a celebration of freedom, equality, and justice. The history of Juneteenth dates back to the end of the Civil War in 1865, when Union General Gordon Granger arrived in Galveston, Texas, and announced the emancipation of all slaves in the state. This was more than two years after President Abraham Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation, which had declared all slaves in Confederate territory to be free. However, it was not until June 19th, 1865, that the news of emancipation reached the ears of enslaved people in Texas. Juneteenth has since become a significant cultural event in America, celebrated by people of all backgrounds and ethnicities. According to a Pew Research Center study, 62% of Americans are aware of Juneteenth, and 43% celebrate the holiday. The significance of Juneteenth lies in its representation of the long and difficult journey towards freedom and equality in America. It is a reminder of the struggles and sacrifices made by African Americans throughout history and a celebration of their resilience and determination. Juneteenth is also a testament to the power of community and the importance of coming together to commemorate important events in American history.
