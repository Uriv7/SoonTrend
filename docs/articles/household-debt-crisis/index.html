What Is a Household Debt Crisis?

A household debt crisis occurs when a significant portion of households struggle to manage their debt, leading to financial difficulties and potentially even bankruptcy. The crisis is often characterized by high levels of debt, poor credit scores, and an increased risk of default. According to a recent study, the total household debt in the United States has reached an all-time high, exceeding $14 trillion. This staggering figure represents a significant increase from just a decade ago, when household debt stood at around $9 trillion. The growing debt burden is putting a strain on household budgets, making it challenging for individuals to make ends meet. In fact, a recent survey found that nearly 70% of households report struggling to pay their bills on time, with many citing debt as a major contributor to their financial stress. The household debt crisis is not limited to credit card debt; mortgage debt, student loans, and personal loans are also contributing to the problem.
